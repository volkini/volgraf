A universal car mat with a pocket moulded into it to hold the water, snow and
sludge that comes in on boots — cuttable to fit any vehicle along perforated
lines.

## The problem

The client had drawings and a cardboard mock-up. Reviewing them raised the
question the concept had not been tested against:

- **Will the water actually get there?** The pocket sits at the seat-side edge.
  Liquid arriving anywhere else has to travel to it, and a mat with a thickness
  difference but no directed path will simply hold water where it lands.
- **Trim-to-fit fights the moulding.** Perforated cut lines have to pass through
  a rubber section thick enough to be durable, without weakening the mat where
  it is not cut.
- **The pocket may not be mouldable in one piece** with the mat, depending on the
  rubber process — which is a manufacturing question, not a design preference.
- **One-handed removal.** The mat has to be liftable, emptied and washed without
  spilling what it collected.

## The approach

- **The flow question raised before modelling.** The client had accounted for a
  thickness difference but had not tested whether liquid reaches the pocket —
  that was flagged, and channels designed to route it deliberately.
- **Directed channelling into the pocket**, so the collection feature works by
  geometry rather than by chance.
- **Perforation lines placed on the sizing envelope** common to cars, vans and
  even farm equipment, so trimming lands in sensible places across the range.
- **Manufacturing route left open.** Whether the pocket is moulded integrally or
  produced separately and assembled was flagged for the rubber manufacturer to
  confirm during production planning.
- **Three iterations**, refining both the channel layout and the trim-to-fit
  concept as the client's universal-fit ambition grew.

## The result

A complete design with drawings for a universal trim-to-fit mat with integrated
liquid pocket and directed drainage.
