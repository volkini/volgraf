Two related products in one brief: a filter that turns any standard water bottle
into a filtered one, and a compact pour-through funnel filter for travel.

## The problem

The bottle filter has to work while someone drinks through it, which makes it a
small fluid system rather than a moulded shape:

- **Draw path.** Water is pulled through an activated carbon cartridge by
  suction, so flow resistance has to stay low enough to drink comfortably.
- **A telescopic breather tube** admits air as water leaves — and it is the most
  fragile part in the assembly. Bottles get dropped.
- **The cartridge is consumable** and needs protecting when the unit is out of
  the bottle.
- **The funnel variant** had a different constraint entirely: fold small enough
  to be worth packing, and clamp onto kettles, coffee machines and mugs of
  wildly different sizes.

## The approach

- **One part, three functions.** The infuser sleeve around the cartridge is the
  key move: it holds tea leaves or fruit when used alone, shields the carbon
  cartridge from drop damage, and braces the breather tube so it cannot bend or
  snap. Combining functions removed parts instead of adding them.
- **Infuser stays, filter is optional.** The architecture lets the user run
  filter plus infuser together, or infuser alone for flavour — resolved
  explicitly with the client, because the original concept had them as
  alternatives.
- **Foldable silicone for the funnel** so it collapses flat, with a clamp
  detailed for the range of vessels the user actually owns.
- **Food-safe materials throughout**, chosen with moulding in mind rather than
  specified after the fact.

## The result

Both concepts modelled and detailed for manufacture across two development
rounds, with the multi-function infuser reducing part count in the bottle unit.

## Where this applies

Small consumer products where a few millilitres of flow, a fragile part and a
cost target all meet. The win is usually one part doing three jobs — but only if
you find it before tooling.
