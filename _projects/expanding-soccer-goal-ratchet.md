A goal that starts small and expands as you crank a handle — from 6×12 ft to
7×16 ft — with the mechanism hidden inside the posts.

## The problem

Telescoping structures are easy to draw and hard to make work:

- **Three members extend at once** — two vertical posts and a longer crossbar —
  and they have to arrive at their end positions together, or the goal racks
  into a parallelogram.
- **A goal takes shock loads.** Whatever holds the extension has to resist a ball
  strike, not just gravity.
- **The mechanism lives inside the tube**, so every component has to fit within
  the post's inner diameter and still be assemblable.
- **The client needed to sell the idea.** The deliverable included animation
  showing the expansion and a cutaway of the internal mechanism — the mechanism
  had to be understandable to non-engineers.

## The approach

- **Ratchet as the holding element**, giving positive engagement at discrete
  positions instead of relying on friction clamps that slip under impact.
- **Extension geometry coordinated across members** so the frame stays square
  through the range rather than only at the two end states.
- **Sections sized for the inner tube**, treating the post bore as the hard
  boundary and designing the drive within it.
- **Animation as an engineering output.** Showing the internal motion forced the
  mechanism to be resolved completely — you cannot animate a mechanism that does
  not work.

## The result

A full 3D model with expansion animation and internal cutaway views, quoted and
delivered as separate design and animation scopes at the client's request.

## Where this applies

Telescoping and expanding structures: goals, stands, gates, frames. Coordinated
extension and positive locking are the two things that decide whether it feels
like a product or a prototype.
