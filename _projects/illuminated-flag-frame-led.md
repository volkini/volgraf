A flag that never sags and lights itself at night — a patented product that
needed to become a manufacturable assembly.

## The problem

The concept is simple and the engineering is not:

- **A flag held rigid is a sail.** Whatever keeps it extended has to take wind
  load through its frame and into the pole.
- **LEDs inside an outdoor frame** need a housing that carries the strip,
  diffuses the light and keeps water out of the electronics.
- **The whole thing has to be assembled** from parts a supplier can produce —
  extruded bar, brackets, end pipes, covers — with a sequence that a person can
  follow.

## The approach

- **Aluminium extrusion as the backbone.** The frame is built around an
  extruded bar section: stiff along its length, cheap by the metre and already
  shaped to accept the LED housing.
- **The LED assembly as a sub-assembly.** The strip, its housing and its cover
  were designed as a unit that drops into the frame, so it can be built and
  tested separately from the structure.
- **Brackets sized for the corner loads**, where a wind-loaded frame actually
  fails, rather than uniformly around the perimeter.
- **Closures on both ends.** End pipes and covers finish the extrusion so the
  frame is sealed against water and looks finished from every angle.

## The result

A full assembly design — frame, LED housing, brackets, covers and end
components — carried through the client's factory review, with answers supplied
directly to the manufacturer's questions on the mechanical schematics.

## Where this applies

Outdoor products built from extrusion and brackets: signage, lighting,
displays, frames. The design is mostly in the joints — what fastens to what,
and what happens there when the wind blows.
