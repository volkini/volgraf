Since EU rules tethered bottle caps to their bottles, drinking from one has
become slightly annoying for everyone. The client wanted to sell the fix.

## The problem

A tool that cuts the tether has to do it in one squeeze, in a pocket-sized
body:

- **The cut is in an awkward place.** The blade has to reach between the
  retaining ring and the cap and shear a narrow plastic band.
- **Nail-clipper kinematics were the reference** — a compound lever with a
  narrowed tip — which sets the force multiplication and the tip geometry.
- **It carries the waste.** The body had to hold roughly three cut caps so users
  can recycle them at home instead of dropping them.
- **It lives on a keyring**, so the blade tip has to be protected or extended
  such that it does not attack whatever shares the pocket.
- **Minimalist, not playful** — an explicit styling instruction.

## The approach

- **Lever geometry from the cut force**, working back from the force needed to
  shear the band to the arm lengths that put it within a comfortable squeeze.
- **A cylindrical body doubling as the cap store**, so the storage requirement
  shapes the product rather than adding a compartment to it.
- **Tip protection resolved as part of the form** — a cover or an extended tip —
  so the safety feature does not look like an afterthought clipped on.
- **Surfaces kept plain**, in line with the minimalist brief, with the visual
  interest coming from proportion rather than detailing.

## The result

A complete product design through two iterations, including a redesign round,
with visualisations for the client to test the concept.

## Where this applies

Small mechanical consumer tools where force, size and safety all compete.
Getting the lever geometry right first makes the styling easy; doing it the
other way round rarely works.
