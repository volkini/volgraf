Most exercise benches are designed for gyms: long, black, industrial and too
large for a flat. This client wanted the opposite — a bench that folds away and
does not look like equipment when it does not need to.

## The problem

Shortening a bench and folding its legs both attack the same property:
stability. A bench is a lever with a person on top of it, and every metre
removed from the footprint costs resistance to tipping.

Three requirements stacked on that:

- **Two clean states** — closed with legs folded in for storage, open and stable
  for use — with nothing fiddly in between.
- **Three height options**, ideally by changing the effective leg length rather
  than adding a separate mechanism.
- **A domestic appearance**: softer forms, not gym black.

## The approach

- **Footprint over height.** The open leg geometry was set to recover the
  stability lost to the shorter body, splaying the contact points rather than
  relying on mass.
- **Captive adjustment pins.** The height pins stay attached during adjustment
  instead of coming fully out — a small decision that removes the most common
  way a user loses a part and the most common pinch hazard.
- **Fold path checked for interference** across all three heights, so no
  combination of settings leaves the legs unable to close.
- **Form developed alongside the structure**, so the softer domestic look came
  from the actual section shapes rather than from covers added afterwards.

## The result

A full CAD design with exploded views and STEP/STL deliverables for prototyping
and manufacture, developed across three iterations with the founder.

## Where this applies

Furniture and equipment that must fold, adjust and still be trusted with body
weight. Stability, adjustment and storage each want a different geometry — the
job is finding the one shape that serves all three.
