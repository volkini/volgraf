A golf bag looks like a sewn product. Structurally it is a rigid frame — a top
cuff, a base, and a set of tubes that keep clubs apart — wrapped in fabric. The
frame is where the design lives.

## The problem

The brief looked simple and turned out to be dense with detail:

- **Club tube layout.** The tubes set how many clubs fit, how they separate, and
  whether the bag stays upright when it is half empty.
- **The opening transition.** Square openings suit the bag's boxy styling, but a
  square edge over a round tube gives an unpleasant catch every time a club is
  dropped in — a detail the user feels a hundred times a round.
- **Handles top and bottom** had to mount into the structure, not into fabric.
- **Fabric cannot be modelled meaningfully.** Textures and weave belong to the
  textile supplier, not to a CAD model — but the manufacturer still needs to be
  told what goes where.

## The approach

- **Structure first, skin second.** Top cuff, tube array and base designed as a
  load path so the bag holds shape whether full or nearly empty; the fabric is
  then detailed onto it.
- **Opening geometry resolved with the client.** The trade-off between styling
  and feel was put on the table explicitly, with the rubber shaft-fixation layer
  between tube and opening taken into account before the shape was fixed.
- **Handles mounted to hard points** integrated into the frame rather than
  stitched to the shell.
- **Notes where models cannot go.** Fabric structure and pattern specified as
  drawing notes for the textile supplier to satisfy, with the brand name and logo
  placed on the front panel.

## The result

Nine design iterations — a heavily revised project — ending in a complete
structural model and a production drawing package covering both the rigid parts
and the instructions the textile manufacturer needed.

## Where this applies

Products that sit between hard goods and soft goods: bags, cases, carriers,
protective gear. Someone has to own the boundary between the frame and the
fabric, and it is usually the mechanical designer.
