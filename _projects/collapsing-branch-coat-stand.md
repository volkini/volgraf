A coat stand shaped like a palm tree, where the fronds hold garments and fold
down when they are meant to.

## The problem

The client's brief was clear about behaviour and vague about how:

- **Two states, one shape.** Fixed, the branches hold a coat. Pressed, they
  collapse together and the coat comes off.
- **The mechanism has to hide inside a decorative form.** A palm trunk is not a
  convenient housing — it is narrow and tapered exactly where a linkage wants
  room.
- **Load direction changes during the motion.** A hanging coat pulls down; the
  release pushes down too, so the mechanism must distinguish between the two.
- **Scale had to be believable.** The client pushed back hard on early proposals
  where the internal parts looked scaled from a reference without regard to the
  actual size of the product.

Much of the project was communication: the client repeatedly asked for section
views and side views to understand the movement, because a mechanism cannot be
approved from a hero render.

## The approach

- **Section and side views as the working language.** Once the motion was drawn
  in section, the conversation moved forward — those views became the primary
  deliverable during development.
- **Linkage sized to the trunk**, not adapted from a reference at a different
  scale, after the client's scale objection.
- **Branch geometry doing double duty**, so the decorative frond shape is also
  the lever arm that carries the garment.
- **Iterated on the movement**, with each version answering a specific question
  about how the branches fold.

## The result

A resolved mechanism and product design carried through the client's review,
with the folding motion documented in section and side views.

## Where this applies

Decorative and furniture products that also have to move. The mechanism has to
fit inside a shape chosen for looks — and be explained in drawings a
non-engineer can read.
