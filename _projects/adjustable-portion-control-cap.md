A cap that fits most condiment bottles and dispenses an exact measure — from a
quarter teaspoon to a tablespoon — with a provisional patent already filed.

## The problem

Universal compatibility and precise dosing pull against each other:

- **Multiple thread standards.** 28-410, 33/400 and 38-400 finishes all had to
  be accommodated, and thread finishes are not interchangeable by eye.
- **Adjustable volume in a moulded part.** A rotating chamber has to define
  repeatable volumes across its range while staying a two-part mould.
- **One-way flow** through a simple valve — simple, because every added component
  raises unit cost on a commodity closure.
- **It must come apart for cleaning** and go back together correctly. Condiment
  residue in a sealed cap is a product failure.
- **Visually intuitive.** Users should see the dose without reading
  instructions.

The client also wanted a vent so the bottle does not lock up under vacuum when
squeezed and inverted.

## The approach

- **Chamber volumes set as geometry, not as markings.** Rotating alignment
  positions define real cavity volumes, so the dose is mechanical rather than a
  printed guess.
- **Base adapter as the compatibility layer**, isolating thread variation in one
  part so the chamber and valve above it stay common across bottle types.
- **Cleaning-driven split lines.** Where the three pieces separate was decided by
  where residue collects, not by what was easiest to mould.
- **Venting handled at the flow path** — a small relief so trapped air escapes as
  a measure fills, which is what makes the dose repeatable in the first place.

## The result

A complete three-piece cap design with the adjustable chamber, valve and
adapter ring detailed for injection moulding.
