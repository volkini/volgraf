The client was launching a folding camping toilet brand. The product folds flat
and transports easily — and that simplicity was exactly the problem: it had no
seat and no urine separation system.

## The problem

Adding function to a product whose selling point is compactness:

- **Separation needs geometry, and geometry needs volume.** A separator is a
  shaped channel; a folding product has almost no permanent internal space to
  put one in.
- **It must survive folding.** Anything added has to either fold with the product
  or come out cleanly and pack alongside it.
- **Cleanability is not negotiable** for this category. Every internal corner and
  radius is a maintenance decision.
- The client's constraint was decisive: **it had to fit the product they already
  had**, not require a new one.

## The approach

- **Design the function as an insert.** Rather than redesigning the folding
  structure, the functional part was designed to sit on top of the existing
  product — preserving the client's tooling and their compactness claim.
- **Separation channel shaped for flow and for cleaning**, with radii sized so
  the part can be rinsed rather than scrubbed.
- **Seat geometry integrated into the same insert**, so one added component
  delivers both missing features instead of two.
- **Mouldable in one piece**, keeping the added part to a single tool.

## The result

A redesigned functional module that fits the client's existing folding toilet,
supplied with drawing and STEP files for manufacture.

## Where this applies

Products that need a missing feature added without invalidating existing
tooling. Designing the addition as a separate part is usually cheaper than
reopening the tool — and always faster.
