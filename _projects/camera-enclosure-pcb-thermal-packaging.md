The client arrived with the hard part already done: a serious custom PCB, a
camera module, and a clear intent to build a real product. What they did not
have was a body to put it in.

## The problem

Enclosure work around finished electronics is constraint-solving, not styling.
Everything was fixed before the first sketch:

- **Sensor alignment.** The camera module had to sit exactly where its optical
  axis needed to be — the housing bends around it, never the reverse.
- **Heat.** The processor needed a heatsink with real airflow across it, and the
  first fan position did not put air where the heat was.
- **Power.** Five 18650 cells had to fit, not four — a late change that moved the
  internal volume budget.
- **Acoustics.** Two board-mounted microphones needed openings that worked
  acoustically without opening a dust path.
- **Travel.** The finished unit had to survive being carried around, not just sit
  on a bench.

## The approach

- **Package from the inside out.** Fixed components placed first — sensor,
  board, cells, heatsink — then the shell drawn around the volume that was left.
- **Airflow as a design feature.** The fan was relocated to blow directly across
  the heatsink fins, with the intake and exhaust path kept clear of the battery
  bay.
- **Custom where it matters, off-the-shelf where it doesn't.** Bespoke sheet
  metal and moulded parts only where the product needed them; standard hardware
  everywhere else, to keep unit cost sane at low volume.
- **Iterate against real data.** Each revision was checked against photographs of
  the assembled board — heatsink height, connector positions, microphone
  locations — rather than against the schematic alone.

## The result

Four design iterations took the enclosure from first layout to a
manufacturing-ready housing with the full electronics stack packaged, cooled and
accessible.

## Where this applies

Any device where a PCB already exists and the product still has to be built
around it — instruments, sensors, portable electronics. Ask for the assembled
board photos early; they contain the dimensions the schematic never shows.
