Suppliers rarely lose an automotive contract because the part is wrong. They
lose it because the submission package is late, incomplete, or fails the
customer's review.

## The problem

PPAP and APQP are not optional for OEM-tier work, and they are not the kind of
paperwork you can improvise near the deadline. A submission needs a control
plan that matches the process actually running, a PFMEA that reflects real
failure modes, measurement system analysis that holds up, and a dimensional
report tied to the right revision of the drawing.

Small and mid-size suppliers usually have the manufacturing capability but not
a dedicated quality engineering desk. The result is a capable shop repeatedly
blocked at the documentation gate.

## The approach

Across 50+ projects in an IATF 16949 / VDA environment I have prepared and
carried through:

- **Control plans** written against the process as it actually runs, not as the
  flowchart wishes it ran.
- **PFMEAs** built from real failure history and process knowledge, with
  actions that a plant will genuinely execute.
- **MSA studies** — gauge R&R and measurement capability, done before the
  dimensional report depends on them.
- **Dimensional reports and full submission packages**, matched to the correct
  drawing revision and customer-specific requirements.
- **Customer audit support** through the review itself.

The work translates directly to remote delivery: it is documentation,
engineering judgement and process knowledge, none of which require standing in
the plant.

## The result

Approvals across 50+ projects, and a documentation process the plant could
repeat without me. Offered now as a **fixed-scope remote package** for
suppliers facing a submission without a quality engineering department.

## Where this applies

If you have won the business and the parts are good but the submission keeps
coming back, the gap is process documentation — and it is a solvable,
fixed-scope problem.
