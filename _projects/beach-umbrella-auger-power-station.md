A beach umbrella holder that drills its own anchor into the sand, powered by a
20,000 mAh battery that doubles as a phone charger, and topped with a table
that folds out.

## The problem

The client had a main body assembly and 2D concept drawings for the next
version. Turning the second into the first meant solving several things at once:

- **A gearbox, motor, gyroscope and PCB** all had to fit inside a body that
  still had to be mouldable — no undercuts hiding in the corners where the gears
  wanted to sit.
- **The auger takes reaction torque.** Whatever twists into the ground twists
  against the user's grip; that load path runs through the housing.
- **The top had to become a tripod-style table**, replacing the existing flat
  mount.
- **The umbrella itself was undefined** — the concept drawings showed 3, 4, 5 and
  7-sided variants, and a mechanism cannot be designed for all of them.

## The approach

- **Pin the variables before modelling.** The umbrella was fixed at the
  four-mount version, based on tent poles and a telescopic stand, so the
  mechanism had a single target.
- **Internals arranged for moulding, not just for fit.** Motor, gearing and
  board were positioned so the surrounding walls could draw cleanly, with wall
  thickness kept even to avoid sink marks on a visible product.
- **Torque path routed into the body**, so the reaction from drilling is carried
  by structure rather than by the battery bay or the cosmetic covers.
- **The gyro question asked early.** Whether a gyro-triggered twist action would
  work reliably in a vertical drilling attitude was raised before it was
  designed around.

## The result

Revised body internals, a redesigned tripod table mount and the auger detail —
delivered as 3D files, renders and manufacturing-ready geometry.
