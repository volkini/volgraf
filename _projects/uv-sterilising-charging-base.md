A charging base for a desk that does two things at once: charges a phone
wirelessly on the outside, and sterilises a removable power bank inside.

## The problem

Three subsystems wanted the same few centimetres:

- **The power bank** had a fixed footprint — the client wanted it dimensionally
  matched to a standard 4.13 × 2.62 × 0.45 in battery pack, so it was an input,
  not a variable.
- **UV sterilisation** only works on surfaces the light reaches. A chamber that
  merely encloses the battery does nothing for the faces in shadow.
- **Wireless charging has a distance budget.** Inductive coupling fails if the
  coil sits too far from the phone, which put a hard ceiling on how much
  structure could go above the coil.

Then the client's team asked for the outer pad to be roughly half the size and,
ideally, retractable from under the box.

## The approach

- **Coil clearance treated as a constraint, not a preference.** The battery bay
  could not be raised, because raising it meant losing wireless charging — that
  trade-off was surfaced early rather than discovered in testing.
- **A sliding door instead of a lid**, so the enclosure opens without needing
  headroom above the unit on a crowded desk.
- **Chamber geometry driven by line of sight**, with the battery held away from
  the walls so UV reaches the faces that matter.
- **Retractable pad evaluated honestly.** A pad that pulls out from under the
  body was assessed against complexity and cost, so the client could choose with
  the mechanism understood rather than assumed.

## The result

A complete design for the base, the sterilising chamber and the matched power
bank, developed across several revisions as the client's team refined the
product's proportions.

## Where this applies

Consumer electronics where a fixed component — a battery, a board, a coil —
sets the rules and the product has to be designed around it. The engineering
value is in knowing which constraint cannot move.
