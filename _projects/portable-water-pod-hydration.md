A 2.5 litre water container that lives beside a desk or a chair, drunk through
a bite valve on a positionable straw, refilled without lifting seven pounds of
water to a tap.

## The problem

The specification mixed materials that do not naturally join:

- **Anodised aluminium centre body, Tritan domes.** Metal and plastic with
  different thermal behaviour, meeting at a joint that must not leak and must not
  show a visible thread.
- **Food-safe sealing** with a replaceable O-ring, because a seal that cannot be
  serviced becomes a disposable product.
- **A refill tube that pops out and extends about a foot**, stored coiled inside
  or outside the body, with a button to release it, a valve to stop drips and a
  path that lets it retract smoothly.
- **A magnetic mouthpiece** on gooseneck tubing that holds its shape, with
  recessed magnets in the base for attachment.
- **Seven pounds when full**, carried by a handle latch that has to be secure and
  removable.

The client also asked whether the whole thing could be double-wall vacuum
insulated aluminium instead — a question worth answering before it was designed.

## The approach

- **Hidden threading at the metal-plastic joint**, so the seamless look survives
  while the joint stays serviceable and the gasket stays replaceable.
- **The insulation question answered honestly.** Double-wall vacuum construction
  is not achievable in this shape at this weight; the trade-off was explained and
  alternatives noted rather than quietly attempted.
- **Refill mechanism designed as a system** — compartment, release button, valve
  and retraction path treated as one problem, since a tube that will not go back
  in is a returned product.
- **Waiting for what was missing.** Where component dimensions were not final,
  the recommendation was to wait rather than design around numbers that would
  change.

## The result

A complete design for body, domes, sealing, straw system and refill mechanism —
including the additional detailing the client requested for the tube storage
container.

## Where this applies

Drinkware and containers combining metal and polymer, especially where sealing
and appearance conflict. The joint is the product.
