A laptop stand is a product people judge in three seconds: it either feels
solid or it does not.

## The problem

The failure modes are all mechanical:

- **Tipping.** A raised laptop puts mass high and behind the pivot. A base that
  looks generous in CAD can still tip when a screen is pushed.
- **Heat.** Lifting a laptop only helps if air can actually move under it — a
  solid deck traps more heat than the desk did.
- **Tilt adjustment** has to hold position under typing force without needing a
  tool.
- **Cost.** This is a commodity category; every gram of material shows up in the
  landed price.

## The approach

- **Slotted deck for through-flow.** The support surface was opened into ribs so
  air passes under the machine rather than pooling, with the ribs oriented to
  carry the load across the span.
- **Base sized against the tipping moment**, not by eye — the footprint follows
  from the worst-case load position, which is a user pushing the screen back.
- **Contact points where the laptop is stiff**, so the machine is supported at
  its structure rather than at the middle of its underside.
- **Material only where load runs.** The visual lightness of the stand comes
  from removing material along low-stress paths, which also removes cost.

## The result

A complete design with production drawings, delivered through two revisions and
signed off by the client's customer.

Desk accessories and supports where the whole product is a load path and a
