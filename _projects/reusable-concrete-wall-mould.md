A client in the Caribbean wanted young professionals to be able to afford a
house. His ICF blocks were stuck in China — shipping cost and vessel
availability had made an imported building system unworkable.

## The problem

The design constraint was economic before it was technical:

- **Whatever replaced the imported block had to be producible locally**, or the
  same shipping problem returns.
- **Cost could not be passed on.** Every design decision that added price
  defeated the purpose of the project.
- **Concrete has to be poured either way**, so the system's value is entirely in
  how easily the form can be produced, handled and reused.
- Candidate materials were **PVC and PP**, with CNC processing as the likely
  production route.

## The approach

- **Interlocking form geometry** so sections align and stack without separate
  connectors — every extra component is a cost the client could not carry.
- **Sections shaped for the process**, keeping profiles simple enough to be
  produced and machined locally rather than needing complex tooling.
- **Reuse designed in.** A form that survives many pours changes the unit
  economics far more than shaving material from a single-use part.
- **The uncomfortable advice given early.** When one proposed route was the most
  expensive option on the table, the client was told plainly — before it was
  designed, not after.

## The result

Two design iterations of a reusable wall form system, with the trade-off between
in-house production ease and unit cost made explicit at each step.

## Where this applies

Construction products and tooling where the real specification is a price per
unit. The design that wins is the one whose production route the client can
actually access.
