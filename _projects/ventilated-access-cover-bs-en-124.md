A UK works contractor used a ventilated lid in place of a conventional duct
cover during site works. It functioned — but it was not built against the
standard that governs it.

## The problem

BS EN 124-3 covers gully tops and manhole tops in steel and aluminium alloys,
and it is not optional on a public works site. The existing lid fell short on
three counts:

- **Securing.** Below a certain mass, a cover has to be positively secured — it
  cannot rely on its own weight to stay put.
- **Identification marking.** The standard requires the cover to carry its own
  identification.
- **Slip resistance.** A defined slip-resistant surface is mandatory, not a
  finish choice.

There was a practical constraint too: the client works in AutoCAD, not
SolidWorks. Deliverables had to be usable on their side without a licence they
did not own.

## The approach

- **Load path first.** The lid was originally modelled in mild steel; material
  and section were revisited against the load class the site actually needs,
  keeping the option open to other alloys that carry the same load.
- **Ventilation without weakening.** The vent pattern was laid out to keep open
  area high while leaving the structural ribs continuous — the perforation
  follows the stress, not the other way round.
- **Compliance designed in, not bolted on.** Securing features, the cast/stamped
  identification area and the slip-resistant surface were treated as design
  inputs from the first sketch.
- **Neutral deliverables.** Drawings and exchange formats the client's own
  AutoCAD workflow and their fabricator could both open.

## The result

A prototype-ready design and drawing package for a compliant ventilated access
cover, produced through three iterations with the contractor's site experience
feeding each one.

## Where this applies

Any product that has to satisfy a published standard as well as a customer.
The engineering is in reading the standard as a set of design inputs early —
retrofitting compliance onto a finished design is always the expensive route.
