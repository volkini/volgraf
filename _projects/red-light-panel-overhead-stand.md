An ER nurse bought a red light therapy panel hoping it would help a family
member's chronic fatigue — and found that every stand on the market either
holds the panel upright, or is too flimsy, too bulky or too expensive to hold
it face-down.

## The problem

Turning a panel to face the floor changes the structural problem completely:

- **The load goes overhead.** A panel above a person lying down must be
  unambiguously secure — this is the requirement that outranks everything else.
- **Existing stands are the wrong shape**, so the design had to start from the
  panel rather than from a stand and be adapted.
- **The panel already has anchor points.** Several of them, which is an
  opportunity: the mounting can use what the manufacturer provided instead of
  clamping the housing.
- **Reconfigurable legs.** The client wanted the rear legs removable and
  re-attachable at the top of the panel so it can be flipped to face downward.
- **It had to be 3D printable**, since this was a one-off for a family member,
  not a production run.

## The approach

- **Mount into the anchor points**, so load transfers into the panel's own
  structure rather than into a clamp that grips a plastic case.
- **A saddle-style bracket** carrying the panel, with the printed legs attaching
  to that saddle — separating the interface from the structure so either can be
  revised alone.
- **Legs designed for print orientation**, with sections aligned so layer lines
  run across the load rather than along a potential fracture path.
- **Symmetric attachment** at both positions, so the same legs serve upright and
  face-down configurations without duplicate parts.

## The result

A complete stand design with printable parts, mounting saddle and
reconfigurable leg attachment.

## Where this applies

Brackets, mounts and stands for equipment that was never designed to be held
that way — especially where the load ends up over a person.
