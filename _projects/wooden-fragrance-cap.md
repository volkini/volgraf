A returning client's fragrance brand wanted a cap that reads as luxury the
moment it is picked up — and specified that it must be wood, all the way
through.

## The problem

The brief was unusually precise, and precision is what makes it hard:

- **Wood only.** No metal insert, no hidden liner. Whatever holds the cap on the
  bottle has to be machined into the wood itself.
- **Dark walnut**, chosen for its natural depth rather than for stain.
- **A ribbed texture of small perforated squares** on the sides — and crucially
  the perforations must *not* pass through. Blind features in wood, at that
  scale, are a machining question before they are an aesthetic one.
- **An indentation on the top** matching the reference photo.
- **Initials in a specified typeface**, centred on the top.
- **A cord attachment** integrated into the design.

## The approach

- **Texture built as real geometry**, not as a surface finish note, so the CAD
  drives the toolpath rather than leaving the shop to interpret it.
- **Feature depth set for the material.** Blind perforations in walnut were
  proportioned so the pattern reads crisply without approaching break-out at the
  thin ribs.
- **Lettering as machined relief** in the requested typeface, dimensioned so
  neither engraving nor embossing loses definition at that size.
- **Thread and cord features integrated into the solid**, keeping the promise
  that there is no metal anywhere in the part.
- **A drawing package alongside the CAD**, because a wood shop needs dimensions
  and finish notes, not just a model.

## The result

A complete cap design with CAD file and technical drawing, delivered across
multiple render rounds until the texture and proportions matched the brand's
intent.

## Where this applies

Luxury packaging and machined natural materials, where the product is judged on
detail quality at arm's length. The tolerance that matters is visual — and it
still has to be dimensioned.
