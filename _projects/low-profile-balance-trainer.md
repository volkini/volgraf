The client was direct about the one thing that mattered: "the profile needs to
be very low — I don't want people tripping over it."

## The problem

A balance device sits on the floor in a space where people also walk:

- **Height is a safety requirement, not a styling one.** Every millimetre of
  thickness increases the chance someone catches a toe on it.
- **It must not slide** when stepped on — a device that moves under load is
  worse than no device.
- **The prototype was rectangular; the product had to be square**, which changes
  the balance behaviour along both axes rather than just one.
- **Dimensions were needed in inches** along both the inner and outer borders,
  since the client and their manufacturer work imperially.

## The approach

- **Structure spread horizontally, not vertically.** With height capped,
  stiffness had to come from the footprint and from the section shape rather
  than from depth.
- **Rubber feet along the base** placed so the device is supported at its
  perimeter where the user's weight lands, rather than at the centre where it
  would rock.
- **Square geometry designed from scratch**, not stretched from the rectangular
  prototype, so the response is symmetric in both directions.
- **Drawn in inches** with inner and outer border dimensions called out
  explicitly, as requested.

## The result

A complete design and dimensioned drawings for the low-profile square device
with non-slip base.

## Where this applies

Floor-level products where safety sets the envelope: mats, platforms, ramps,
trainers. When one dimension is fixed by a hazard, every other decision follows
from it.
