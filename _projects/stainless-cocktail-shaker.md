A bar shaker that pours through a lever-operated valve instead of being
cracked open — which turns a simple vessel into a sealed mechanism that has to
survive a dishwasher.

## The problem

Every requirement pointed at material and sealing:

- **Food safety throughout.** Body, valve, strainer and seals all had to be
  specified as food-contact materials — 304 stainless for the body, food-grade
  silicone for seals and grips.
- **The valve has to seal against liquid under shaking**, then open cleanly on a
  lever.
- **The bottle opener needed reinforcement** — thicker 304 so repeated levering
  does not deform it.
- **The second strainer had to detach without twisting off.** The client was
  explicit: it should slide in and out, which rules out the usual threaded or
  bayonet answer.
- **Citrus and repeated washing** attack seals and crevices, so anywhere liquid
  can sit is a design defect.
- Late in the project, **two extra measure sizes** (0.5 oz and 0.75 oz) were
  added to the small shaker.

## The approach

- **Slide-fit strainer retention** using a captive rail rather than a thread, so
  the part comes off in one motion but cannot rotate loose in use.
- **Grooves as moulded geometry.** Stirring and measuring grooves were placed to
  survive the extra measure sizes without redrawing the vessel.
- **Silicone confined to serviceable seals**, sized so they can be removed and
  replaced rather than being trapped where cleaning cannot reach.
- **Rubber base pad integrated** into the small shaker to stop it walking on a
  bar top.
- **Sequenced priorities.** The client ranked lever and strainer above other
  features and the cylinder valve first of all; work followed that order rather
  than the model's convenience.

## The result

A full assembly across three iterations, with materials specified per component
and the valve, strainer and opener detailed for manufacture.

## Where this applies

Food and beverage hardware where sealing, cleanability and material compliance
all apply at once. The mechanism is the easy half; the seal and the wash cycle
decide whether it survives.
