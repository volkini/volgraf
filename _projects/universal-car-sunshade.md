Existing foil sunshades do not cover the whole windshield and do not stay up.
The client wanted a product that fixed both.

## The problem

The obvious approach — a magnetic frame gripping the car's trim — does not
survive contact with a real vehicle:

- **Magnets have nothing to hold.** Around a windshield, the surrounding trim is
  usually not ferrous. A magnetic design fails on most cars in the target
  market.
- **Every windshield is a different shape.** A universal product cannot match the
  curvature of each one.
- **Full coverage is the whole point.** Partial coverage is what makes existing
  products annoying — light comes in at the corners.

The useful insight came from the client's own research: while shapes differ,
**overall windshield dimensions repeat** across many models.

## The approach

- **Design to the dimension, not the shape.** The panel was sized against the
  repeating envelope so it covers the opening on a wide range of cars without
  trying to follow any single curve.
- **Attachment reconsidered from the start**, once magnets were ruled out on the
  physical evidence rather than after a prototype failed.
- **Coverage at the edges prioritised**, because corner gaps are the specific
  failure users complain about.
- **Two iterations**, with the concept revised as the client refined how the
  product should look and pack away.

## The result

A complete design for a universal sunshade with the attachment approach
resolved and the sizing logic documented — the product's actual claim being
based on a measurement, not on a hope.

## Where this applies

Aftermarket accessories that must fit many vehicles. The design question is
always which dimension is common enough to build a product on.
