A device that connects the ear canal to the cheekbone: a stethoscope-style ear
piece at one end, a small pad resting on the cheekbone at the other, and the
problem that no two faces are the same.

## The problem

Anything worn on the head runs into human variation immediately:

- **Ear-to-cheekbone distance varies** by more than a fixed part can absorb, so
  adjustment is not a convenience feature — it is required for the device to
  function at all.
- **The cheek pad must maintain contact** at a consistent angle across that
  range, or the connection it is making is inconsistent.
- **Pressure has to be gentle but positive.** Too little and it slips; too much
  and it is unwearable within minutes.
- The client's own concept was **three pieces**: an ear piece looping to
  mid-cheek, a middle section, and the cheek pad on a small hinge.

## The approach

- **Detented slide adjustment**, using the same principle as a headphone
  headband: discrete positions that hold under load and can be set without
  looking.
- **The hinge at the pad**, so the contact surface self-aligns to the cheek
  regardless of where the slide is set — adjustment for length, articulation for
  angle.
- **Three-part architecture kept**, since it lets one moulded family cover the
  size range instead of needing multiple part sizes.
- **Iterated on fit.** The design went through four versions, each refining the
  adjustment range and pad geometry.

## The result

A complete four-iteration design with production drawings, covering the ear
piece, middle section and hinged cheek pad.

## Where this applies

Head-worn and body-worn devices — medical wearables, audio, monitoring — where
anthropometric variation is the main constraint. Adjustment mechanisms are
where these products succeed or get returned.
